🪳 Creepy-crawlies splash page!

People often use the word “bugs” to describe all kinds of creepy-crawlies, but true bugs are actually a specific group of insects. This page explains the major types.

🐞 Beetles (Coleoptera)

Beetles are the largest group of insects, with hundreds of thousands of species. They have hard wing covers that protect their bodies.

Some beetles are helpful, like ladybugs that eat pests, while others can harm crops.

Image by URSchmidt – CC BY-SA 4.0

🪲 True Bugs (Hemiptera)

True bugs include insects like stink bugs and aphids. They have mouthparts used for piercing and sucking.

Some true bugs damage plants, while others prey on pests.

Image licensed under CC BY 3.0

🦋 Butterflies & Moths (Lepidoptera)

Butterflies and moths have colorful wings covered in scales. They go through full metamorphosis.

As adults, they help pollinate plants and support ecosystems.

Image by Didier Descouens – CC BY-SA 4.0

🦟 Flies & Mosquitos (Diptera)

These insects have only one pair of wings, which makes them excellent flyers.

Mosquitos are known for spreading diseases, while flies help break down waste.

Image licensed under CC BY 3.0

🐝 Bees, Wasps, Ants (Hymenoptera)

These insects often live in social colonies and have complex behaviors.

Bees are vital pollinators, while ants help improve soil health.

Image by Trounce – CC BY-SA 2.5

🕷️ Spiders (Araneae)

Spiders are not insects. They have eight legs and produce silk.

Most spiders are harmless and help control insect populations.
